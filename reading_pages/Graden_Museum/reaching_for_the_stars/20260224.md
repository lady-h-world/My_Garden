## Where's the Bottom Line of Trust?

I'm going to fly to SF on Mar. 1st, early morning. So this is my last week. Today suddenly got a message from the buyer who ordered most of my furnitures decided to cancel all of them. Can't sell all these large furnitures in a few days, now I have to give them away in much lower price and even to give away for free. Worst case will be to spend money to donate.... 

Last month, when I was interviewing with TSMC, the interview with Taiwan team was the most disrespectful experience ever all these years. They asked me to prepare 15 min presentation and 45 Q&A 3 days before the interview. I spent the whole Saturday to prepare for that. At that time I had to do full time job, deal with moving plans, building my AI products, interviewing with other companies. But during the interview, the interview didn't allow me to present at all even after I asked in the first 30 min, "Could you let me present first, it can provide much more details to your questions.", they didn't allow without any reason, also they kept asking requestions only related to my experience in 4+ years ago, not relevant to their job description at all, my recent 2 years experience strongly align with their job description, and my experience is the rare one with production and evaluation experience. After the interview, 2 weeks later, they rejected me through system rejection. This company is so disgusting!

When people are doing these, who can we trust?

Recently, Anthropic assused Chinese AI companies distilled their AI answered into Chinese LLMs' training data. Some Chinese comment "this is normal behaviour in business world". But where's the bottom line of such behaviors?

It's human nature to be dishonest when we have chance, because human beings are greedy, in order to get more value with smallest cost, human beings play all kinds of tricks. Without rules, laws, constraints, where's the bottom line and where's trust?

I still hate TSMC so much!
